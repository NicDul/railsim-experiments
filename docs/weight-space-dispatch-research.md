# Weight-space programming for Railsim dispatch: feasibility study

**Scope:** research and a future experiment plan only. No controller code was changed, no model weights were edited, and no simulator experiment or benchmark was run. This exploration is separate from the current capacity/flexibility optimization work.

## Executive recommendation

Weight-space programming is worth a **small, later test-driven spike**, but the article does not establish that a frontier model can discover a better Railsim dispatch policy just by editing weights. Its strongest direct transfer is narrower: a general model may be able to encode a small, well-specified decision rule into a fixed, tiny network. That is different from learning a superior policy from simulation outcomes, and the reported work shows that iterative feedback and evaluation can still be extensive even when gradient training is absent.

The Railsim source suggests a plausible mechanism for the reported symptom: its default disposition checks resource availability at the decision time and may reroute at a marked entry-to-exit segment when a route resource is currently unavailable. It does not compare the blocker’s expected release time with this train’s expected arrival before choosing a detour. That is source evidence for a hypothesis, **not** a reproduction of the user’s case.

Recommendation: first build and compare an interpretable time-aware heuristic against the existing reactive rule in a tiny isolated test. If that still leaves a useful policy gap, try a compact frontier-authored policy with the same observations and actions. Keep the frontier model offline; use a deterministic local policy at runtime. Do not start with a full RL/imitation-learning effort or put a frontier API in the simulator’s hot loop.

### Evidence limits and the Railsim analogy

The authors’ log repository says it contains runs reported in the article but omits earlier prompt trials; its “tool-free” label disables Codex tools, not the separate Python or forward-pass harness described in the experiment logs. [Repository scope and tool setup](https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs/blob/4d09ba6d6a7e891dd8063974dfe81b3d78e3494f/README.md#L1-L13), [tool/harness details](https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs/blob/4d09ba6d6a7e891dd8063974dfe81b3d78e3494f/README.md#L28-L57) These are author-run records, not independent replications. The Qwen edit is reproducible from its public diff and pinned base weights, but the separate H200 rerun does not provide a full matched base-model rerun. [Rebuild code](https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs/blob/4d09ba6d7a6e891dd8063974dfe81b3d78e3494f/exp8_math/code/apply_edit.py#L1-L13), [rerun qualification](https://huggingface.co/RikiyaT/Qwen3.5-0.8B-weight-space-programming/blob/main/README.md#L35-L64) The official Qwen model card warns about thinking loops; the experiment uses temperature 0.6 while the card recommends 1.0 for that mode. The base/edit comparison keeps settings matched, but the result is still specific to this model and evaluation setup. [Model-card warning](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-0.8B/blob/2fc06364715b967f1860aea9cf38778875588b17/README.md#L855-L857), [experiment sampling protocol](https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs/blob/4d09ba6d6a7e891dd8063974dfe81b3d78e3494f/exp8_math/code/evaluate.py#L31-L45) The experiment records `gpt-6-astra`, but I found no provider-signed evidence that independently verifies service-side model routing. No claim is made that an available frontier model, including a different model ID, can reproduce those results.

The relevant distinction is **program synthesis into weights** versus **learning a better policy**. Tracr is a primary precedent for compiling a known program into transformer weights; it does not show that a model can discover a better railway policy. [Tracr paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05062) The article’s “fast weight programmer” reference concerns learned fast-weight memory updates, not arbitrary static checkpoint editing. [Fast-weight programmers paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11174) Conversely, MeZO is a forward-only zeroth-order method and illustrates that removing gradients does not remove optimization. [MeZO paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17333) The Qwen demonstration also uses iterative measurements and feedback. The article’s “no training” claim therefore should not be read as “no search, compute, feedback, or validation.” Weight Oracles and NeuroGen are specialized weight-reading/generation approaches, distinct from a general frontier model used as-is. [Weight Oracles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07334), [NeuroGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12470) The benchmark setting also matters: MinAtar is deliberately a simplified grid-game environment, while Kinetix is a physics-based RL benchmark; their results do not directly establish generalization to multi-train dispatch. [MinAtar paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03176), [Kinetix paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23208)

For Railsim, a fixed small policy is much closer to the structured game examples than to editing arbitrary pretrained checkpoints: the state variables, legal route candidates, and action meanings can be specified in advance. The possible result is an authored compact decision circuit; whether it outperforms a transparent heuristic, generalizes across schedules, or improves delay/throughput is unknown until it is tested.

## 2. Railsim’s actual decision path and extension seam

The project’s [current public baseline](https://github.com/NicDul/railsim-experiments/commit/aa035fc4a3b9cce0142ac076ca1ae87c20242fb8) matched `origin/master` when checked. The project pins MATSim and the `railsim` contribution through version `2026.0-PR4828` in [`pom.xml`](../pom.xml#L4-L8) and [`pom.xml`](../pom.xml#L78-L85). I inspected the public [upstream PR #4828 merge commit](https://github.com/matsim-org/matsim-libs/commit/0284097e84dbab0ad4c8a9e7ffda985739898410) source for the dispatch findings below. The version label corresponds to that PR, but the exact published Maven source JAR was not independently compared; this is source inspection, not a runtime reproduction.

The local wrapper installs `RailsimModule` and `RailsimQSimModule` in both the one-run path and batch `RailsimSimulationJob`; it does not currently bind a custom dispatch policy. [`RunSingleSimulation`](../src/main/java/org/matsim/project/RunSingleSimulation.java#L86-L99), [`RailsimSimulationJob`](../src/main/java/org/matsim/project/simulation/RailsimSimulationJob.java#L104-L109) The upstream [QSim module](https://github.com/matsim-org/matsim-libs/blob/0284097e84dbab0ad4c8a9e7ffda985739898410/contribs/railsim/src/main/java/ch/sbb/matsim/contrib/railsim/qsimengine/RailsimQSimModule.java) explicitly binds `TrainDisposition` to `SimpleDisposition` and notes these interfaces may be replaced. The existing one-run code already demonstrates replacing `DeadlockAvoidance` through an overriding QSim module. This makes a custom `TrainDisposition` the natural future integration seam, though it must be wired consistently into the standalone and batch simulation paths.

### Why the described reroute is plausible—but not reproduced

In the inspected upstream [`SimpleDisposition`](https://github.com/matsim-org/matsim-libs/blob/0284097e84dbab0ad4c8a9e7ffda985739898410/contribs/railsim/src/main/java/ch/sbb/matsim/contrib/railsim/qsimengine/disposition/SimpleDisposition.java), rerouting is considered around `railsimEntry`/`railsimExit` links. For a route segment between those markers, `reroute(time, ...)` looks for a segment link that is not already reserved by this train and has no capacity **at the current `time`**. If it finds one, it asks `TrainRouter` for a detour and submits that candidate to `RailResourceManager.checkReroute`. The [`TrainRouter`](https://github.com/matsim-org/matsim-libs/blob/0284097e84dbab0ad4c8a9e7ffda985739898410/contribs/railsim/src/main/java/ch/sbb/matsim/contrib/railsim/qsimengine/router/TrainRouter.java) is a Dijkstra-style path finder with capacity-based link costs and checks prohibited turn sequences; its cost is not a predicted route travel-time comparison.

The [`RailsimEngine`](https://github.com/matsim-org/matsim-libs/blob/0284097e84dbab0ad4c8a9e7ffda985739898410/contribs/railsim/src/main/java/ch/sbb/matsim/contrib/railsim/qsimengine/RailsimEngine.java) requests a route reservation ahead of movement. Its reservation distance is tied to stopping distance and the next stop; if capacity is unavailable, the train can be stopped and the request retried. Thus, for marked entry/exit routes, a currently unavailable route resource can plausibly cause a detour without asking whether it will clear before this train reaches it. That matches the user’s explanation as a **candidate mechanism**. I did not run the case, inspect a particular event trace, or establish that the specific user-observed reroute has this cause.

The repository includes entry/exit markers in its UC1 station network, and its UC1 BB1 config sets both the blocked-track `pollInterval` and train `updateInterval` to 1 second. [`network.xml`](../src/main/resources/scenarios/use_case_1/building_block_1/network.xml#L45-L94), [`config.xml`](../src/main/resources/scenarios/use_case_1/building_block_1/config.xml#L20-L28) The decision cadence is nevertheless event-driven: the disposition is consulted when the engine requests more reserved distance, with blocked reservation attempts retried on the configured poll interval. The one-second config values are not a promise that a complete global dispatch optimization runs once per second.

## 4. Proposed architecture

Separate the **offline designer** from the **runtime controller**:

1. Define and test a small causal observation/action contract against the Railsim event/state APIs.
2. Generate a short list of simulator-legal candidate routes and estimate the time to the conflict and the blocker’s release interval. Missing or uncertain information must be explicit, not treated as “free.”
3. Use a readable temporal heuristic as the first policy. If it leaves a measurable gap, let a frontier model author a small fixed weight vector/network offline, using only development cases.
4. Store the exact policy code, architecture, prompts, weight file, and hashes. The simulation process loads the frozen weights and executes deterministic local inference. No frontier API is called in the Railsim hot loop.
5. A Java adapter maps the policy’s preference back to `TrainDisposition`. Existing route generation, `RailResourceManager` reservations, `DeadlockAvoidance` checks, and Railsim movement physics remain authoritative.

For a first tiny network, use roughly 8–16 normalized inputs, one hidden layer of about 8 units, and three scores (`keep`, `hold`, `detour`), on the order of 100–170 parameters. This is a proposed architecture, not a requirement or result. A linear policy with fewer weights is an even simpler baseline.

| Feature/action item | Source or implementation need |
|---|---|
| Current time; own head/tail link and position; route index, next stop, delay, train length/type | Mostly available through `TrainPosition`/`TrainInfo`; speed needs the train-state event or a confirmed state-observer seam. |
| Current resource capacity/occupancy and reservation owner | Query current capacity with the resource manager; reconstruct owner/state from link-state events or expose a read-only snapshot. Do not infer a future release from a current boolean. |
| ETA to entry/exit and predicted blocker release interval | Estimate from current train positions/speeds, route, length, braking, stops, and minimum headway. Carry a lower/upper interval and an “unknown” flag; validate the estimate against events before using it. |
| Candidate route cost and constraints | Generate candidates with the router; score added distance/time and conflict resources. Mask candidates that violate route continuity, turn restrictions, stops, reservations, or deadlock checks. |
| Priority/fairness/memory | Add own waiting age, previous holds/reroutes, and a resource-level age/last-grant tie-break. Railsim’s public position interface alone does not provide a global dispatch queue. |
| `KEEP`, `HOLD`, `DETOUR(candidate)` | A policy preference only. The adapter validates and applies it; the engine still performs the reservation. Invalid output, NaN scores, missing state, or an empty candidate set must be counted and fall back to the existing deterministic disposition (or a bounded hold) rather than bypassing constraints. |

Do not treat the policy as a signal/interlocking system. Preserve physical route legality, route reservation, capacity, and deadlock checks outside the learned function. A hold must be bounded and revisited so that two trains do not wait indefinitely; aging and a stable deterministic tie-break help with crossing priorities and starvation. Partial observability should result in a conservative baseline/fallback, not invented future state.

## 5. Smallest informative experiment to approve later

**No experiment is run as part of this report.** After explicit approval, start with a tiny isolated network or timetable built around the existing Railsim entry/exit and resource concepts, not the capacity/flexibility optimization loop.

Create four behavior families:

1. **Clear on arrival:** at the decision instant, the exit/conflict resource is occupied or reserved; the blocker’s tail clears (including any minimum headway) before the test train is predicted to arrive. A legal detour exists. This directly tests unnecessary rerouting.
2. **Still blocked:** same topology and nearly identical timing, except the blocker’s release is later than the test train’s arrival. The train must not enter an unreserved resource; holding or a legal detour can both be acceptable depending on the measured wait-versus-detour cost.
3. **Crossing priority:** two trains approach a shared single-capacity crossing/resource from conflicting directions with controlled arrival offsets and delay. Verify legal reservation, deterministic tie-breaking, and no repeated priority flip.
4. **Hold/fairness:** a train waits for a resource with no useful detour, then the blocker clears; include a variant with a long block. Verify progress after release, bounded waiting, no route oscillation, and no starvation of the later train.

Use paired timing variants so the clear-on-arrival and persistent-block cases differ only in the blocker’s release time relative to the test train’s conflict arrival. The first comparison should include four arms: existing `SimpleDisposition`; a time-aware heuristic; a frontier-authored small policy with the same inputs/actions; and an ablation of that policy without the time-to-clear feature.

**Proposed pilot budget (not measured):** use 8 development cases (two variants per family) and 20 locked held-out variants (five timing/seed variants per family). Four policy arms produce 32 development and 80 held-out episodes, 112 runs total. The frontier model and heuristic may be adjusted only on development cases; weights and prompts are frozen before the held-out runs. Five held-out variants per family are a screening sample, not enough for a broad performance claim; if the pilot passes, expand to 20 or more paired seeds/variants per family before concluding that a policy generalizes.

For a fair comparison, hold network, scheduled trains, random seed/timing, simulation horizon, policy observation fields, legal candidate list, and evaluation budget constant across arms. No policy receives future occupancy or exact release labels unavailable at decision time. Use actual later release times only in the evaluator to classify an unnecessary reroute, never as a feature. Give the heuristic and weight-authored policy the same number of development simulation calls; cap frontier weight-edit iterations (proposed: 5–10) and record every prompt, returned weight vector, run, seed, and config hash. Keep the final test seeds locked and do not feed their outcomes back into either policy.

Report, separately rather than hiding them in one reward:

- terminal delay distribution (median, p95, maximum), plus not-started and unfinished trains;
- completed trains/throughput by the fixed horizon;
- reroutes per train and **unnecessary reroutes**, labelled post hoc when the original resource actually clears before the train’s conflict arrival;
- deadlock/no-progress episodes, each train’s maximum continuous wait, and starvation events;
- invalid or rejected action rate and route/resource legality violations;
- policy inference p50/p95 and total simulation runtime.

Do not optimize delay alone: an agent could appear fast by leaving trains unfinished or refusing to enter a congested network. Keep incomplete trains and throughput visible in every score; count invalid actions as failures; record reroutes and no-progress independently; use the same event-time basis for all policies.

**Proposed screening targets, not measured facts:** zero illegal/invalid actions; no new deadlock or starvation in these finite cases; zero unnecessary reroutes on the scripted clear-before-arrival case; and no entry into a still-unreserved conflict resource in the persistent-block case. A deadlock alarm could be defined for this pilot as no forward-position or reservation progress for 60 simulated seconds while active routes remain; a starvation alarm could be a train waiting more than 60 seconds and more than three times its free-flow conflict traversal while another train passes the same resource. These are pilot definitions, not railway standards.

For a later “continue weight-space” gate, propose requiring at least a 10% reduction in unnecessary-reroute rate versus the existing rule on locked variants, with p95 delay no more than 5% worse, throughput no more than 1% lower, and no hard-constraint regressions. These percentages are provisional thresholds to make the decision falsifiable, not evidence-based performance guarantees. A small pilot can justify a larger test, not a claim of optimality or zero delay.

### What can only be learned by testing

Reading source cannot establish whether the user’s exact reroute is caused by this path, whether time-to-clear is predictable enough to improve decisions, whether the added state can be observed cleanly in the pinned dependency, whether the heuristic will trade waiting against detours better, or whether frontier-authored weights generalize to held-out traffic patterns. The previous team’s model, data, reward, and evaluation are not present in this repository, so its reported poor results remain unexplained.

The right question for the first experiment is not “can a frontier model edit weights?” The article provides author-run evidence that it can sometimes encode compact rules. The falsifiable Railsim question is: **with identical causal observations and legal actions, does a tiny offline-authored policy reduce avoidable detours beyond a temporal heuristic without worsening delay, throughput, fairness, or legality on locked cases?** No result in the article answers that question.
